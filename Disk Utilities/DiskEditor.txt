        When I first got a PC (The LEGENDARY C-64), I was facinated with
computers and programming became my NEW hobby. I studied the C-64 like it
was a book, and that is where I learned all the programming concepts that
help me become the great programmer I am today. When I got my first IBM
compatable, it was really easy to port over the skills I earned from
my C-64.  But some things just were not there. For one, the C-64 had
sprites, where the IBM, is more where you have to program your own
sprite routines. Another thing is THIS: Disk Edit v1.0 .   On the old
C-64 they had all kinds of Track/Sector Disk editors all over the place.
And I tell you, you could edit a disk, and change sector pointers, directories
, filenames, delete files, and just do all kind of things that could even
help you protect your software.  Well now you can do the same for the IBM.
Of course there are major differences such as FAT and Boot tables, but in
time you can learn them.  As I am writing this, I still need to do a lot of
reading on the BOOT SECTOR and other stuff. I have 2 tables below that
may help you out a little.  Either way, it is utilities like this that need
to be distributed, so that people have more control over their data storage
devices. - DICEMAN / Corey Badeaux


USAGE HELP:
        Ok, the commands are simple and should be really easy to use and
understand if you have fooled with a sector editor before.        
Just use the appropriate keys to advance to the next track/sector, jump
to a distand t/s, write over sectors, copy data from one sector/drive to
another, and even a pretty good editor for the sectors you are in.

Remember the following when viewing disk drives:
        1.44 floppies have 80 tracks/ 18 sectors/ 2 heads (0,1)
        720K floppies have 80 tracks/  9 sectors/ 2 heads (0,1)
 Hard Drives can have different numbers of tracks/sectors and usually have
 16 heads. Maybe in future versions I will just auto-detect these stats.
